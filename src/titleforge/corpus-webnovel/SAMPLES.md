# New-pattern sample output (for QA review)

## dungeon-anchor  (weight 3)
_The dungeon-core naming convention: a one-word modifier welded to Dungeon or Core. Instantly legible as POV-is-the-dungeon fiction._

Exemplar: *Cultivating Dungeon*

- The Certified Dungeon
- The Semi-Retired Dungeon
- The Level One Dungeon
- Verdant Core
- The Unranked Dungeon
- The Chronically Underpowered Dungeon
- Hollow Dungeon
- Cinder Core
- Verdant Dungeon
- Bone Dungeon

## possessive-relation  (weight 4)
_The protagonist named by who owns them or what they hold. Two registers share one shape: the fantasy/dungeon streak (the alliterative "The Crafter's ...") and the huge binge-romance-app streak ("The Alpha's Mate", "The CEO's Contract Wife"). Crossed the gate once the corpus was scaled; the romance-app bloc is what gives it its dominant register._

Exemplar: *Carl's Doomsday Scenario*

- The Heir's Daughter
- The Necromancer's Grudge
- The Alpha's Apprentice
- The Alpha's Rejected Mate
- My Archmage's Bride
- The Ceo's Cookbook
- The Warden's Cookbook
- My Warden's Ledger
- My Tyrant's Apprentice
- My Tyrant's Regret

## regression-rebirth  (weight 3)
_The rebirth marker is the whole premise: the reader is promised a protagonist who already knows how the story ends. The single most load-bearing frame in anglophone web fiction after the bare class-role._

Exemplar: *I Became a Patron God*

- Transmigrated into the Bureaucrat
- Becoming the Apprentice Lich
- Reincarnated as the Dungeon Inspector
- Reincarnated as the Quartermaster
- The Tavern Owner Who Lived Twice
- Reborn as the Bureaucrat
- The Second Life of the Rooster
- Return of the Reluctant Respawn Clerk
- The Retired Villain Who Kept the Patch Notes
- The Bureaucrat Who Came Back Wrong

## bracket-tag  (weight 3)
_The bracketed genre tag welded to the title — a Royal Road discoverability move (readers filter by tag, so the tag goes in the title). Barely exists in trad prose; a native web-serial artefact. Crossed the gate once the RR tracker data was folded in._

Exemplar: *Godless Sword [Timeloop LitRPG]*

- The Reluctant Farmhand [An Idle Litrpg]
- The Provisionally Immortal Apprentice Lich [A Cozy Apocalyptic Litrpg]
- The Level One Dungeon Inspector [Kingdom Building]
- The F-Rank Quartermaster [Progression Fantasy]
- Verdant Dungeon [Litrpg]
- The Level One Portal Technician [Litrpg]
- The Provisionally Immortal Dungeon Core [Kingdom Building]
- Tutorial Meadow [Progression Fantasy]
- Cinder Dungeon [A Cozy Apocalyptic Litrpg]
- Cinder Dungeon [Kingdom Building]

## interrogative-hook  (weight 2)
_A question or aside addressed straight to the reader. Signals comedy and a low-solemnity register; the hook is tone, not stakes. Sits badly next to the sincere Wattpad register — keep them apart._

Exemplar: *Awakened As A Dungeon Core?*

- Why Is Everyone Obsessed with the Bureaucrat?
- Turns Out I'm the Apprentice Lich?
- So What if I'm the Retired Villain?
- So What if I'm the Innkeeper?
- So I'm the Reluctant Accountant?
- What Do You Mean I'm the Bureaucrat?
- So I'm the Rooster?
- So I'm the Provisionally Immortal Portal Technician?
- Why Is Everyone Obsessed with the Semi-Retired Librarian?
- Why Is Everyone Obsessed with the Accidental Summoner?

## status-hook  (weight 2)
_The relationship-status hook that dominates the binge-romance apps (Dreame, GoodNovel, NovelCat): the title states what was done to the heroine by the love interest — rejected, married, kidnapped, sold. Fated-mate and contract-marriage machinery compressed into a headline. Firmly the sincere register; keep it away from the ironic frames._

Exemplar: *Kidnapped by My Mate*

- Abandoned by the Don
- Claimed by the Beta
- Rejected by the Warlord
- Rejected by the Luna
- Bound to My Alpha
- Mated to the Billionaire
- Bound to the Billionaire
- Bound to My Billionaire
- Betrothed to My Warlord
- Betrothed to My CEO
