# storyForge

storyForge is becoming a standalone desktop app (Tauri 2, macOS first). This repository holds the app's **data core** (a plain TypeScript library over a folder of markdown, no dependency on Obsidian) and its **Tauri shell** (a Rust side that owns the disk, a file watcher, settings and app life) and its **v1 interface** (plain TypeScript and DOM, no framework) with the **numbers** (the series and novel overviews and the word-count history, read-only) and **writing sessions** (recorded when they end, shown in the history view). The design decisions live in the project's `storyForge app – decisions.md`; read it before changing anything structural. The Obsidian plugin continues, unchanged, as `storytellingForge`; its last storyForge-repo incarnation (with its interface) is the git tag `plugin-harness-final`.

Reports to read first: `docs/strip-report.md` (what was removed from the plugin and why), `docs/project-handle.md` (the design of the core, with the review rulings in §10), `docs/data-core-report.md` (what was built, and every difference from the plugin's behaviour) and `docs/safety-core.md` with `docs/safety-core-report.md` (the prose writer, recovery, backups, reconciliation, open/create and link rewriting), then `docs/tauri-shell.md` (§12 rulings, §13 as built) and `docs/tauri-shell-report.md`, then `docs/interface.md` (§12 rulings, §13 and §14 as built) and `docs/interface-report.md`, then `docs/numbers.md` (§11 rulings, §12 as built) and `docs/numbers-report.md`, then `docs/sessions.md` (§10 rulings, §12 as built) and `docs/sessions-report.md`, then `docs/story-shape.md` (§12 rulings, §13 as built) and `docs/story-shape-report.md`, then `docs/typing-aids.md` (§12 rulings, §13 as built) and `docs/typing-aids-report.md`, then `docs/look.md` and `docs/look-report.md`, then `docs/look-2.md` (§7 rulings and as built) and `docs/look-2-report.md`, then `docs/stats.md` (§9 rulings, §10 as built) and `docs/stats-report.md`, then `docs/widths.md` (§9 rulings, §10 as built) and `docs/widths-report.md`, then `docs/navigator.md` (§11 and §14 rulings, §12 to §14 as built) and `docs/navigator-report.md`, then `docs/settings-sync.md` (§ Rulings and § As built) and `docs/settings-sync-report.md`, then `docs/novel-2.md` (Brief 14, the novel tab round two: § Rulings and § As built), then `docs/data-bars.md` (Brief 15, the data bars: H1 rulings and § As built), then `docs/series-2.md` (Brief 19, the series page: H1 and § As built).

## Layout

- `src-tauri/` — the Rust side. `fs/` (`storyforge-fs`, no Tauri dependency): `guard.rs` (the Rust twin of the TypeScript guard; passes `test/guard-spec/guard.json` unchanged), `containment.rs` (no write through a symbolic link that leaves the project), `ops.rs` (atomic writes, compare-then-write with `renamex_np` exchange, exclusive create/rename, `.trash/` retire, scan), `api.rs` (the one dispatch every command goes through), `watch.rs` (batching), `machine.rs`, `format.rs`, `platform.rs` (the only macOS-specific code). `src/lib.rs` is the window glue; `capabilities/main.json` grants the window only this app's own commands (`test/tauriConfig.test.ts` enforces it).
- `src/backends/tauri/` — the TypeScript Backend over those commands. `src/app/` — settings (`project.md`), `applyBatch.ts` (the watcher's batch order), `appLife.ts` (open, create, schedule, close). `sessionRecorder.ts` (one per open project: listens to the author's edits, ends a session at its last edit, checkpoints to the app's data folder, writes the session file when a session ends, finishes a crashed session at the next open). `src/diagnostic/` — the developer's diagnostic layer (development builds only; compiled out of a release).
- `src/ui/` — the interface. **Panel names:** the left panel is *storytelling | library* (a flip-sided panel; storytelling is where the app opens, library holds the codex, series, novel and archive tabs), the centre is the *workspace*, the right is the *context* panel. In `src/ui/`: `kit/` (buttons, menus, dialog, notices, inline rename), `theme/` (purposes and the one palette file), `editor/` (CodeMirror, the dressing, the save notices, the two editor slots), `faces/` (storytelling and storyForge), `overviews/` (the layer over the workspace on the storyForge face: series overview, novel overview, the data-bar primitives, debounced refresh), `context/` (the context panel on the library side's novel tab: `chapterContext.ts`, the chosen chapter's PoV, Location, plot note and read-only text, and `chapterText.ts`; the special-characters button), `screens/` (`history/` the word-count history dialog; start, new project, the reconciliation summary, Codex renaming, settings), `menu.ts` (the menu items the window answers), `model.ts` (what the interface reads from and acts through), `preview/` (the browser preview's page; `scripts/preview/plugin.ts` is its development-only server).

- `src/core/` — the library. `project.ts` (the **Project handle**: a synchronous in-memory index for reads, guarded asynchronous writes with write-through), `projectIndex.ts`, `frontmatter.ts` (parse and minimal-diff edit), `backend/` (the Backend interface, the **write guard** and `GuardedBackend`), `proseWriter.ts` (the single prose write path), `recovery.ts`, `tempTidy.ts` (leftover temporary files at open), `backup.ts`, `snapshot.ts`, `reconciler.ts`, `openProject.ts` (open and create), `linkRewriter.ts` and `linkScan.ts` (Codex rename), `sessions.ts` (the session tracker, pure, the clock passed in), `sessionFigures.ts` (the three kinds, pace and the storytelling figures, worked out when shown), `sessionFiles.ts` (the session files), then the ported data layer: `paths`, `series`, `book`, `codex`, `codexTree`, `tagRegistry`, `chapterSidecar`, `storyDetailsSidecar`, `chapterCreation`, `chapterPlacement`, `history`, `reconciliation`, `sectionBody`, `coverImage`, plus the pure maths and helpers.
- `src/backends/node-fs/` — the file-system Backend (atomic writes, `.trash/` retire). Used by tests and the CLI check now; the Tauri backend is Brief 4.
- `src/view/` — DOM-only helpers kept for the interface work (`dragReorder`, `dragReorderTree`, `dragLock`, `a11y`, `codexOpenTarget`) and `src/layout.ts`, `src/storytellingCenter.ts`. No Obsidian dependency.
- `src/cli/` and `scripts/` — the developer check: `npm run check -- <folder>` copies a folder and reports on it; it never writes to the source.
- `test/fixtures/` — synthetic projects in the 0.17 layout (`node test/fixtures/build.mjs` regenerates them; commit the result). `test/guard-spec/guard.json` — the write-guard rule table the Rust guard must also pass. `test/diskSafety/` — the disk-safety suite.

## Hard rules

- **Interface rules:** the interface reaches the core only through its APIs: prose is written only through `ProseSession` (never from a view), Codex renames go through `OpenedProject.renameNote`. **No colour outside `src/ui/theme/`**: components use named purposes (`--purpose-*`); `src/ui/__tests__/purposes.test.ts` fails on a hex, `rgb(` or palette variable anywhere else. Copy is British English in the plugin's lexicon (`docs/text-labels.md`). New dependencies need a ruling; the interface's are CodeMirror 6, Lucide and (development only) Playwright. A new Rust command may only read or reveal, or set the app's own menu items; **the exception (decision 121, 1.9.0) is the app's own recent list: `remember_project`, `forget_recent` and `clear_recent` may write `recent.json` in the app's data folder, and nothing else** (every command about the recent list takes an index or a position or nothing, never a path; `peek_recent` and `peek_recent_book` only read). The command table is `docs/tauri-shell.md` §3.

- **Writing sessions** (docs/sessions.md): only the author's own edits in a chapter editor count (through `ProseSession.setBody`; never reloads, link rewrites, reconciliation or Codex notes). The project folder is written **only when a session ends**, and only under `_backstage/storyforge/sessions/` (one file per month per computer, named with a random id, never the Mac's name); the session in progress lives in the app's data folder (`session/<sha>`). Typing never waits on any of it. `wordcount.md` is never touched by session tracking. Editing and short are worked out when shown, from the settings; unreliable (a paste or drop adding over 30 words) and recovered are stored. Unreliable and editing sessions are left out of the storytelling figures (the history view's days, weeks and heatmaps, and the stats panel's today and week), never out of chapter, novel or series counts or any bar. A session file that can't be read is never rewritten: the next session goes to a sibling. Ending the session is a step in the close path, after the editors flush and before the snapshot, and counts as watchdog progress.
- **The overviews and the history view write only the author's own words and choices** (docs/story-shape.md). Opening any of them writes nothing (a test compares the project folder byte for byte). What can be written from the overviews and the context panel: planned lengths (the shared `lengthFields.ts`, set in the left pane's novel tab: the workspace no longer shows them), the plot note of the **chosen chapter** (in the context panel), the novel's synopsis and the series description (through `FieldState`/`textField`, committed on blur and on every close route, never per keystroke), the chosen chapter's PoV and location lists (through the Codex picker, in the context panel; the default PoV is set in the left pane) and covers. Every field write is compare-then-write against what the field last saw (`core/fieldWriter.ts`, the comparison inside the same lock as the write); a conflict, a refusal, a throw or a close that cuts a commit off puts the author's text in a recovery file (plot notes under `recovery/<book>/` as `<chapter> (plot note)`, synopsis and description under `recovery/_fields/`), never only in the field. A section write (`## Synopsis`, `## Description`) matches only a line that is exactly the heading, refuses a duplicated heading (the field is then read-only) and changes only that section; an empty field removes it. **The chapter cards are bars only** (Brief 14): the three card sizes are gone, and the stored `cardTiers` (a machine-settings key from 1.6 and earlier) is carried through every machine-settings save exactly as read, never interpreted, cleaned or dropped (`MachineProjectState.cardTiers` is `unknown`). **Only placed chapters** are shown in the novel overview. The overview is a layer over a still-mounted editor, which is `inert` while covered. **Chapter bars** (Brief 15, `docs/data-bars.md`; `overviews/bars.ts`): a solid 34 px bar (4 px radius) in the **data colour**, inside a rail (`--purpose-data-frame`, 42 px, 8 px radius, concentric; **Fix 11, decisions 153 to 155: a background colour, `bg1` to `bg4`, `bg2` by default, the no-cover box's fill; the setting `rail-colour`; a stored grey or data colour draws the default and is never rewritten; its contrast is under 3:1 on bg_dim, accepted by Kenny, and the target is in the tooltip and name**) whose inner width is the chapter's planned length: short of the target the grey left over is the shortfall; at it (decision 28's rounding) the bar meets the frame's inner edge; past it the bar runs on, solid and rounded, out of the frame's end. The target is shown by a shape, so overrun never relies on colour alone. **Every frame has the same width** (`chapterScale`: the longest chapter's share of its target, 1 at the least and **3 at the most**, so the frames are the row divided by it and **never under a third of the row**; the frame's padding is not scaled, so the longest bar ends exactly at the row's end; **a chapter past 300% runs to the row's end with a cut end**, a V bitten out of it with 3 px rounded corners, its tooltip and name giving the true target), so their ends line up; the bars' geometry is CSS custom properties (`--sf-inner`, `--sf-barw`, `--sf-frame`), so a resize redraws nothing. **No words draws no fill at all, anywhere (no sliver; a planned length keeps its rail); any words written draw at least 3 px (`min-width` on `.sf-bar-fill`), and the progress bar's whole fill is at least 3 px with its segments true to scale; the names stay `bg_dim` over the fill and the rail (decision 158)**; **no chapter length means no frame** (a plain fill scaled to the longest chapter). There are no dots, marker, clearance layer or text stroke, and `--purpose-data-overrun` is retired. The name is one colour (`--purpose-text-on-data`, bg_dim), 19 px at 700, no outline, lower middle, cut short with "…" at the further of the frame's inner end and the bar's end, less 8 px; never on the bare page. **The data colour is a project setting** (`data-colour`: `yellow`, `aqua`, `blue`, `orange` or `purple`, stored as the role name, default blue; red, green and unknown values draw blue and are never rewritten; Settings ▸ Appearance; set on the page as `data-data-colour` and mapped to `--purpose-data-fill`), for the chapter bars, the series bars and the novel progress segments. The history heatmaps keep blue through their own purpose, `--purpose-heat-base`. The series overview's bars (`plainBar`, 12 px, a fill only, none for a novel with no words) are unchanged. Ghost chapter dividers are drawn only where adjacent ones would be at least 4 px apart at the bar's measured width (`computeGhostChapterDividerPercents`' minimum spacing), redrawn on resize. Net-loss days are never red.
- **Covers** go through two Rust commands: `pick_cover(target)` (the target is the series or a book code, never a path; Rust shows the open panel, accepts PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP by their first bytes up to 10 MB, writes under a temporary name, retires any existing `cover.<ext>` to `.trash/`, always, and moves the new file in create-only) and `read_cover(target, filename)` (read-only, exact `cover.<ext>` names, 10 MB). TypeScript sets or clears `cover-image` and retires the file on removal; there is no `clear_cover`. Only `ui/model.ts` calls `pickCover` and only `ui/overviews/cover.ts` calls `readCover` (architecture test). Covers are drawn from `blob:` URLs, so the content security policy's `img-src` is `'self' data: blob:` (a test keeps it in step with the sources the interface uses); the preview serves no policy, so `npm run test:cover` (Mac only, built app) checks they are really drawn. A cover that is recorded but can't be shown says so in a notice. **A cover is a button**: with one recorded it opens a small floating menu, Change Cover… and Remove Cover (no confirmation: it is recoverable from `.trash/`); with none, clicking it chooses one. The series cover works the same way.
- **The chosen chapter, the context panel and the backgrounds** (Brief 14, `docs/novel-2.md`): **one chosen chapter, `nav.chapter`, is shared by both sides.** `model.chooseChapter(book, file)` (a click on a bar or on a row of the left pane's novel list) switches the one chapter editor by the same save-and-open route as `openChapter` but with `EditorHost.open(…, { reveal: false })`: the library side keeps its view (the overview stays up, the editor beneath stays `inert`) and no focus moves. The chosen chapter's row is always highlighted (the "Highlight the active chapter" setting is **retired**: no setting, no checkbox; a stored `highlight-active-chapter` key is left unread and never rewritten). **On the library side's novel tab the context panel shows the chosen chapter** (`ui/context/chapterContext.ts`): PoV and Location on the panel, the plot note (the field editor of decision 47, on a card) and the chapter's text **read-only** (`chapterText.ts`): selectable, never an editor, **showing the open session's current text** through `EditorHost.watchText`/`liveText` and following it live, **never connected to the prose writer or the session tracker** (an architecture test: no import of the writer, the chapter editor, the host, the tracker or the session files; it asks the host only for `watchText` and `liveText`). Changing the chosen chapter commits every focused field first (`commitFocusedFields`, to the chapter it belongs to), so typed words are never dropped with the old field. **Backgrounds:** the whole library side is `bg_dim` (the left panel, the workspace, the context panel; the strip under the title band too, on the library side only); only the places where you work are cards on `bg0` (10 px corners, no border): the synopsis, the series description, the context panel's plot note and chapter text, the Novel tab's placed and unplaced lists (`.sf-chapter-list`, each its own card; the series and archive panes' lists are not) and a Codex entry or archived item in the library workspace. The storytelling side is unchanged (the chapter editor stays `bg0`). **Hints** (placeholders, the Codex list's "Empty" line) are grey2 at full strength, no extra opacity (4.61:1); "No cover" (3.99:1 on `bg1`) is an accepted exception, like decision 110's muted rows. **Icon buttons turn the accent (green) on hover**, never the text colour (`defaultRules.test.ts`): the Navigate chevron, the fold chevrons, every `.sf-button--icon`; the Codex strip's icons keep the Codex colour (decision 67).
- **A Codex rename re-points references:** chapters' PoV and location lists and the default PoV are matched on the stored path (case and Unicode folded, never the name), updated in the form they already have, left alone when a note is archived, pruned or moved out (they show as missing), and a novel file that can't be rewritten is skipped and listed (`rekeyCodexNoteReferences`).
- **The close commits fields first:** `controller.registerCloseCommit` / `commitOnClose.ts`. A field being edited registers while it has focus; the close commits them before the editors flush, sends `close_progress`, and if a commit outlasts the limit calls its `rescue`. Planned lengths are the one exception where a lost number at quit is accepted (decision 42). `npm run test:quit` runs every route with text typed in a chapter and in a plot note, confirms the text reached the field first, and reports "not typed" retries separately from a loss.

- **Typing aids** (docs/typing-aids.md; smart punctuation, name autocorrect, shortcodes, the special-characters button): they act **only on text being typed right now**: the character just typed and at most the word it finishes, and only inside the current unbroken typed run (`typedRun`). Never on text that was already there, or that arrived by paste, drop, dictation, composition, undo, redo or a reload, and **never inside `[[links]]`, URLs, code, comments or link targets** (`core/typing/protectedText.ts`). Each keystroke's change is **one isolated undo step** (`isolateHistory`), so one ⌘Z gives back exactly what was typed; it is made through the browser's own editing (`execCommand("insertText")`, recorded by `inputHandler`) so WebKit keeps its spelling underlines (a change made through the CodeMirror state makes WebKit forget every underline in the paragraph). The rules are pure functions in `src/core/typing/` (with a fuzzer: nothing outside the run or inside protected text, every change undoes to what was typed, the word count never changes); the editor extension is `src/ui/editor/typingAids.ts` and the popup `shortcodePopup.ts`. **The Mac's own smart quotes, dashes, Text Replacements and autocorrection are off for the editor** (`platform.rs`, `register_editor_text_defaults`, per-app, in memory); the red spellcheck underlines are on. Settings are `typing-*` keys in `project.md`. `npm run test:typing` (Mac, built app) types real keystrokes and checks the result, ⌘Z through the Edit menu, a shortcode with the real Tab key and the button; `--field-shot` / `--shot` save pictures of the underlines.
- **Plot notes, the synopsis and the series description are small plain-text CodeMirror editors** (`ui/overviews/fieldCommitter.ts`, decision 47), not text areas, because a text area's undo can't keep an aid's change as its own step. They take all the typing aids on the same options as a chapter (decision 53: smart punctuation, name autocorrect and shortcodes; the shortcode popup is drawn in the page's body so nothing clips it, goes when the field loses focus, and Tab accepts only while it shows). The rename, settings and search boxes take none (`typingAids` is mounted only in `editor.ts` and `fieldCommitter.ts`, an architecture test). They have their own history (cleared when the field is refreshed from disk), and are **never connected to the prose writer or the session tracker** (an architecture test); the Edit menu's Undo and Redo reach the focused field through `commitOnClose.ts`.
- **The look (Brief 10, `docs/look.md` and `docs/look-report.md`):** SN Pro (unmodified upright and italic variable files and `OFL.txt` in `src/ui/theme/fonts/`, recorded in `Font Licences.md`) is the font of the chapter and Codex-note editors, the three fields and the Codex list, through `--font-prose` (`"SN Pro", Arial, system sans-serif`: Arial, with a real italic and bold, draws only what SN Pro lacks). The chapter and Codex notes are 20 px at 1.6, with a 30em maximum width. Ligatures and `ss01` are off and font synthesis is off on those surfaces (what is on screen is what is on disk). It is loaded from the app (`font-src 'self'`); `npm run test:look` (Mac, built app) reads the real line widths and the slant of wynn in italic to prove it. **The Codex list** (`faces/codexTree.ts`, one list on both sides) has no chevrons, icons 1em after the name (none on an untagged note), a folder opening and closing with the mouse only by its 28 px icon, lines beside open folders, an "Empty" line, tree roles with one tab stop and the standard keys (↑↓ → ← Return; reordering is ⌥ plus the arrows), and a strip down its left edge (New Folder, New Note) that names the new thing in place: **nothing is created until Return**, Escape creates nothing, and the rename's rules apply (plus no clash with a note, or a folder in the same parent, ignoring case and accents). **The context panel** shows a note on a card (margin `max(12px, 10%)` of the panel's width) above an always-present bottom row. **The special-characters button** (`editor/lastWriting.ts`) is always shown and puts its character, as typing with its own undo step, into the editor last written in (the chapter, a note, a plot note, the synopsis, the description) at that editor's own selection; it is greyed when that editor is covered, read-only or gone; the tracker's test hook does not exist in a production build (`test/releaseBuild.test.ts`).
- **The look, round two (Brief 11, `docs/look-2.md` and `docs/look-2-report.md`):** **Scrollbars are hidden at rest everywhere** (`scrollbar-width: none`, whatever the Mac's own setting); `kit/scrollIndicator.ts` (installed once in `startApp`) draws a thin bar in the page body while something scrolls and fades it after a second (no fade under reduced motion); it never takes a click. **No dividing lines** between the app's parts (the window's outer line is the Mac's own frame; the library rail's right border is transparent, not removed, so the ink indicator stays; the splitter shows only the resize cursor on hover and the green line on keyboard focus). Control outlines, focus rings and state markers stay. **The Codex list's colour is one purpose, `--purpose-codex`** (text, icons, folder lines, the strip's icons and the highlight), set by the project setting `codex-colour` (`text` the default, `yellow`, `green`, `aqua`, `blue`, `purple`, `orange`, `grey`: not red or the backgrounds; decision 67 amends decision 8 for the Codex list only; applied to the page as `data-codex-colour` by `startApp`; an unknown value is the default and never rewritten). The list is 16 px; the selected row and the row under the pointer are a block in that colour with the panel's colour on it; a folder's line is flush with its name and a nested row's highlight starts just right of it; there is no "Codex" heading (the tree is still named "Codex"). **A folder that is also a note is drawn once**, as the folder's row: the view skips a child whose path is the folder's own linked note (`shown()` in `codexTree.ts`). Display only; the entry stays in `codex.md` and a reorder keeps it where it is (a core test). A nested row's highlight has a straight left edge and a folder's line runs from the top of its first child's box to the bottom of its last. The strip's two buttons are hover icons: only the icon's colour changes (`--purpose-codex-rest`, which is grey0, at rest whatever the Codex colour; the Codex colour on hover and keyboard focus); the focus ring on a Codex row is `--purpose-codex-ring` (the text colour when green is chosen). On the storytelling side the project name and the novel's title are the accent colour as text only (`data-face` on `.sf-left`).
- **The stats panel** (Brief 12, `docs/stats.md`, decisions 68 and 69; `faces/stats.ts`): one row, the graph icon (24 px, opens the history view), the icon of the count on display (16 px, a button that steps **day, week, session, chapter, novel**) and its number. Only the way the figures are shown changed: day and week are still the storytelling figures, session is `live().net`, chapter and novel as before. **Which count shows is navigation state** (`statsCount` in the machine settings, per project; day isn't stored): cycling never writes `project.md`. **With the live readout switched on, a second row is always drawn** (clock icon, 16 px, directly beneath the count icon in its column, the space beneath the graph icon left empty, and the count icon's bottom edge level with the graph icon's: **clock** with no session, **clock-bill** while one is ongoing, **clock-pause** when paused; the session's length in hours and minutes only, `0m`, `25m`, `1h 05m`; its words, left out while session is the count above). The length is first edit to last edit (`SessionRecorder.live()` reports `lastEditMs`, read-only), so it holds still while paused, and **the clock is the paused one from a minute without an edit** (`core/sessionReadout.ts`, `PAUSE_AFTER_MS`) until the next edit or the session's end: a visual reminder only, nothing recorded differently. **The whole section is `grey1`** (`--purpose-stats`, about 4.1:1 on `bg_dim`, Kenny's choice). Every icon in the section is Tabler (MIT; Kenny's choice, 1.6.0): the graph (24 px, filled) and, at 16 px as 2 px outlines, calendar-due (day), calendar-week, calendar-clock (session), file-text (chapter), book (novel), and the clocks clock, clock-edit (a session ongoing) and clock-pause (`kit/statsIcons.ts`; `Icon Licences.md`). The count icon is a real button: its name and tooltip say what shows and what is next ("Words today; press for this week"), and the number is a polite live region naming what it counts.
- **Panel widths** (Fix 5, `docs/widths.md`, decisions 76 and 77; `ui/panelWidths.ts`, `screens/widthsSettings.ts`): three widths (the left panel on each side, the context panel), a same-width switch (on by default; per view since Brief 19) and a unit (percent by default; the unit choice is gone since Brief 19), **all machine settings per project, never `project.md`** (`leftWidth` is the storytelling side's; `libraryWidth`, `loreWidth`, `widthUnit`, `widthsLinked`). Limits in pixels: left 200–640, context 280–640, shared 280–640, workspace 360. **A window too small for the set widths narrows the panels for display only** (`displayWidths`): the stored figures are never rewritten. Typed figures are committed on Return or blur and refused outside the range; settings stays modal.
- **The navigator and Appearance** (Brief 13, `docs/navigator.md`, decisions 81 to 94): eight embedded families since 1.10.0 (Aleo, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, Courier Prime, Fraunces, SN Pro, Sofia Sans in three widths; Inter left in 1.10.0; `core/fontFamilies.ts`, `Font Licences.md`), chosen per part (Codex list, novel title, series name, selected chapter) in Settings ▸ Appearance as `appearance-<part>-font|weight|size` (and `-soft` for Fraunces; Wonk was removed in 1.6.1: it is always set off, and an `appearance-<part>-wonk` key on disk is never read or rewritten) in `project.md`, sizes in rem and drawn as px by `APPEARANCE_BASE_PX` (the page root is 13 px, so CSS `rem` is never used). An unknown or out-of-range value reads as the default and is never rewritten. `story-kind` (`series` or `standalone`, default series) decides whether the storytelling navigator shows a series line. The navigator's series and novel names open their library tabs; the chapter dropdown opens over the Codex card; "Add the next chapter" appends. **The storytelling side only:** the library side is unchanged. On the storytelling side the Codex list is on a card (line height 1.1; both cards end 12 px above the stats section, which is a fixed `--stats-h`, 64 px, readout on or off).
- **The series page, the novel selector and the one gap** (Brief 19, `docs/series-2.md`, decisions 140 to 143): the **library's chosen novel** (`nav.libraryBook`, session only) is shared by the series tab, the novel tab and the context panel, and **choosing one never changes `nav.book`, the chapter or the editor** (`model.chooseNovel`); it is cleared only when the novel being written changes (`setWritten`), not when another chapter of the same novel is chosen. ## Hold rules (stop and report, don't push on)

- A YAML construct can't be rewritten minimally.
- A test can't be ported faithfully.
- Compare-then-write can't be made atomic on a file system.
- A 0.17.1 behaviour being ported turns out different from how the brief describes it.
- Anything would add a file, folder or key to the project (the session snapshot lives outside it).
- Something would need a behaviour change in a kept module, not just a severed import.
- The disk-safety suite fails and the fix would widen what an operation touches.
